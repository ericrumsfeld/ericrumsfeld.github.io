NBA 2026-27 Standings Predictions

Created on October 20, 2026

2026   ·   work

On the eve of what is slated to be the NBA’s most exciting regular season to date, how do I forecast the teams to finish when all is said and done? For the first time in a long time, the Eastern Conference projects to be stronger than the West, with LeBron James and Kawhi Leonard heading east, and Giannis Antetokounmpo and Jaylen Brown joining forces with budding superteams. Will the Knicks experience a championship hangover? Should we doubt the Nuggets as a playoff lock? Which team(s) will surprise us all and take the NBA by storm as the Spurs did last season? Find out now in my NBA 2026-27 Regular Season standings predictions, with nuanced modeling for off-season moves, roster continuity, team identity, and injury luck.

(Note: Correctly predicting NBA regular season standings with high accuracy is very difficult! Only three games separated the 5 from 10 seed in the East last year. That’s what makes the NBA so much fun to follow. I believe one could reasonably place the Eastern Conference Seeds 1 through 8 in any given order. The Western Conference 3-6 Seeds and 7-13 Seeds could also be in any order without a complaint from me. To highlight the tiers better, and to allow an excuse for myself when my predictions are inevitably imperfect, I have listed teams in 4 tiers in each conference.)

Western Conference


Western Tier 1: Championship Favorites


1. San Antonio Spurs [Last Season: 2 seed, 62-20]

Of course, the Spurs’ success rides on the shoulders of their 7’4 giant, Victor Wembanyama. Posting the 4th best offense and 3rd best defense last season, with fantastic continuity and a clear identity, the Spurs will have another immensely successful season if Wemby can stay healthy. This team is young, always developing, always feisty, and their frontcourt depth has been strongly bolstered through their off-season moves. The franchise has a clear intent to put physicality around Wemby, raising their floor, and Dylan Harper’s seemingly endless potential gives me optimism for a high ceiling. Although each team in the Southwestern Conference should be improved from last year, potentially stealing a few more wins from the Spurs (last year they went 13-3 against division rivals), I think the talent and system here is too great to ultimately deny their top spot in the West.

Notable Departures A+

  • None... yet.

Notable Additions A−

  • Tobias Harris provides a super reliable veteran presence on both ends of the court. Quaintance and Reed Jr. add significant depth at the four/big-man position.
  • Quaintance and Reed Jr. add significant depth at the four/big-man position.

Roster Continuity A+

  • Kept their most important players: Wemby, Fox, Castle, Harper, Champagnie, Johnson, Barnes, Bryant, and Kornet.
  • Lost Olynyk, Sochan, and Biyombo, but these aren't particularly notable departures. Overall, excellent roster continuity.

System & Coaching A+

  • It's easy to win games when you have a 7'4" defensive demigod breaking out as a potential future GOAT candidate. The Wemby-centric system features feisty role players who create disruptions on both ends of the court.
  • Mitch Johnson (CBS Rank: 9) had some potential missteps in last season's playoffs, but winning so many games without Wemby during the regular season gives me confidence that he can correct those mistakes in just his second year in charge.

Injury Projection C

  • Tobias Harris suffered a calf strain during training camp that may keep him out of the season opener. Hopefully, this doesn't linger into the regular season.
  • Quaintance may not join the team on the court until midseason, even under an optimistic projection.
  • One would expect their great injury luck last season to regress toward the mean, potentially creating more challenges than they experienced last year.

2. Oklahoma City Thunder [Last Season: 1 seed, 64-18]

Trust me, I do feel guilty putting one of the youngest teams, who were on pace to break the regular season wins record last season until injuries held them back (and still conquered the league with the best record in the NBA anyways) in the 2nd Seed position. They have the two-time reigning MVP, got back all-star Jalen Williams, and one could reasonably project ever greater development from Cason Wallace, Chet Holmgren, and Jared McCain. This roster is feisty, incredibly well-balanced, has a clear identity and fantastic coaching, and absolutely no one would be surprised if they hit on their draft selections again. Name one weakness they have. No matter the opponent, they should rightly feel they would win on any given night. That being said, they did lose a number of winning players to shed salary they spent on Isaiah Hartenstein in the form of Lu Dort, Isaiah Joe, and Aaron Wiggins. This, and my belief in Wembanyama as a transcendent NBA talent are the only two reasons I’ll give the razor-thin margin in favor of the Spurs as a bold prediction.

Notable Departures C-

  • Lu Dort
  • Isaiah Joe
  • Aaron Wiggins

Notable Additions B

  • Jared McCain only played 30 games last season due to being traded halfway through the season. I expect a much bigger role from him.
  • Nikola Topić only played 10 games last season after undergoing testicular surgery and chemotherapy. I expect a much bigger role from him. (Edit: Topić has been dealing with a back issue that's keeping him out indefinitely as the season starts... not what anyone wants to hear.)
  • Aday Mara and Bennett Stirtz could both potentially be ready-to-go impactors in defined roles, but I wouldn’t expect them to rise up to record-changing players immediately. Having Thomas Sorber this season for depth could be a strong swing factor, though. (Edit: Sorber is out indefinitely to start the season as he recovers from a minor knee surgery).

Roster Continuity B

  • Kept their most key players (Shai, JDub, Chet, Caruso, Cason, Hartenstein, McCain). Having Ajay Mitchell, Jaylin Williams, and Kenrich Williams return is nice. Lost some key contributors who played tons of minutes including Dort, Joe, and Wiggins.

System & Coaching A+

  • Very well defined around star players and defensive/transition ecosystem where role players know exactly how to create positive impact. I trust Mark Dagineault (CBS Rank 2) a lot.

Injury Projection A-

  • Isaiah Hartenstein only played 47 games last season due to calf/lower leg injury, but the Thunder shedded multiple important contracts this offseason to keep him for another 3 years, clearly investing in his interior physicality, reliable offensive and defensive presence, and ability to act as a glue guy.
  • JDub only played 33 games last season due to wrist and hamstring injuries last year, I’d project he plays a lot more this year which should help a lot.
  • Overall, last season they were quite unlucky with injuries last season as many projections had them winning 73+ games at the start of the year. A return to average would mean they experience more luck but there are some lingering doubts with Topić and Sorber out to start the year.

Western Tier 2: Dogfight of Respectably Good Teams


3. Los Angeles Lakers [Last Season: 4 seed, 53-29]

Welcome to the Luka show. The Lakers have transitioned off of the LeBron James era and rapidly constructed a Luka-centric roster. Walker Kessler is a drastic improvement on what the Lakers were rolling out last season as he should provide fantastic rim protection on defense and all-time scoring efficiency offensively. Although this looks like a completely new roster, they are bringing in a ton of defensive-minded, win-now, experienced NBA players which raises their floor. The offense is not the side to worry about. Building an effective defense around Luka and Austin Reaves is quite the challenge, but JJ Redick seems to be up to the task as he hopes to instill consistent defensive intensity and discipline.

Notable Additions A

Roster Continuity D+

  • Kept only a few core guys (Luka, Reaves, LaRavia, Vanderbilt), but mostly it’s going to be a lot of new faces that have to try to adopt a defensive identity on the fly. Offense should be good with Luka and Reaves as stars, but defense is largely unproven and there are huge question marks for exactly how this team will look.

System & Coaching B-

  • JJ Redick (CBS Rank 8) seems like quite a solid coach, we’ll really see this season, though as he says he now has a roster he can actually do something with. LeBron-sized vacuum means this will be a Luka & Reaves team (strong offense, no defense) but the ecosystem around them is totally up to JJ’s design so hard to forecast.

Injury Projection B

  • Kessler only played 5 games last season after being shut down with shoulder injury, I think we can reasonably expect 60 out of him but I would not bet on 75+.
  • Luka and Reaves both average ~65 GP per year, should likely expect something around there again.
  • Team has some slight star injury concerns, but depth seems solid enough to continue on.

Adding Fred VanVleet’s 3-point shooting and steady playmaking will be a great relief for everyone on this team. I am mostly optimistic that the Rockets will have a great season, although I would bet we can’t expect 38 year old Kevin Durant to play 78 games again this year. Yes, the Rockets lose some relevant depth, but their strong off-season and overall continuity should be enough for this team to maintain their success from last year.

5. Denver Nuggets [Last Season: 3 seed, 54-28]

Another season, another round of disappointment from the Nuggets front office. What’s new? The Nuggets scraped into the 3 seed last year due to the force of all-star Jamal Murray and Peyton Watson carrying the team during injuries to Nikola Jokić and Aaron Gordon, and a late winning streak against tanking teams. Although the starting lineup has reached outstanding success, able to battle anyone on their day, another year older means we can continue to expect injury misfortune to hold them back. The Nuggets desperately needed 3-point shooting and DEFENSE. none of which was addressed in their off season moves. I hope Alpha Diallo translates well and becomes an NBA defensive stalwart. I like DeMar as a steady offensive option off the bench, I’ll let myself be pleasantly surprised by Tyus, Marvin, and Lonnie, and I think Trevon Brazile projects as a dynamic, athletically imposing future option with enough time to develop an NBA game. Ultimately, the Nuggets possess a core on the tail end of excellence and retooling around Jokić may be necessary if they want to reach the peaks they did in 2023 (especially if they want to beat the TWolves in the playoffs).

System & Coaching A-

  • The Jamal - Jok - AG trifecta is one of the most tried and true systems in the NBA, regularly posing the most lethal offense when healthy.
  • Adelman (CBS Rank 23) is not a generational coach, but job is easier when you have the basketball mind of Jokic. We need true genius to figure out how this roster is going to play any defense, though.

6. Minnesota Timberwolves [Last Season: 6 seed, 49-33]

The Timberwolves took a big swing trading away Julius Randle, Naz Reid, an unprotected first-round pick and 3 second-round picks for all-star Lamelo Ball. Lamelo provides clear value to the Twolves as one of the most talented offensive engines in the league, commandeering playmaking duties from Anthony Edwards as he’s been wishing for so long. Although the Lamelo + Ant combination is perfect on paper, likely the best backcourt in the league, I expect they will show elite flashes of success throughout the year, but only translate that to win dominance later in the season. It takes time to merge two star guards, especially if they’re both as high usage as these two (both >30%, top 17 in the league last year). I like the flier on Kuminga, but I doubt he alone will be enough to bolster the physicality and size that was crucial to their identity last year.

9. Portland Trail Blazers [Last Season: 7 seed, 42-40]

The Trailblazers were a surprise team last season to some (personally, I was more optimistic than most) owing to the breakout of Deni Avdija and defensive consistency from Toumani Camara and Donovan Clingan. Clearly, there will be a huge shakeup with the addition of two time all-star Ja Morant and similarly high-usage Damian Lillard. Starting both of those offensive-minded, small guards puts heavy pressure on that defense which was 14th in DRTG last season. With the reintroduction of their franchise legend alongside a host of young players to develop, there’s a lot for first time head coach Micah Nori to figure out rotation-wise. Although, I see talent and creativity giving this roster another surprising, competitive edge to unsuspecting teams.

Notable Departures C

  • Jerami Grant
  • Kris Murray
  • Matisse Thybulle
  • Shaedon Sharpe tore his right lateral meniscus during an off-season workout, keeping him out until at least late February at best. He’s a young guard/wing they’d love to see leaps from (no pun intended), but with their current guard depth in excess, it may not be worth rushing him back.

Notable Additions B+

  • Damian Lillard
  • Ja Morant
  • Micah Potter

Roster Continuity C-

  • Adding two undersized former all-star guards potentially to the starting lineup gives this roster a completely new look, and a headscratching conundrum of how to make it work on court.
  • Much of the core from last year remains, and the trailblazers should hope for the same success from Avdija and Clingan to continue their playoff hopes, although the 3 guard lineups are confusing and doubtful in my mind to find much success.

System & Coaching B-

  • Micah Nori (CBS Rank 27) is, in my mind, a very underrated coach for a first time head coach. Of course, he had some success as the stand in for Chris Finch during the 2024 playoffs, but I think he has shown some talent for on-court strategic decision making. He has a huge task to figure out this seemingly disjointed lineup with overlapping strengths at the guard position, as well as some personality management with Deni just having the best season of his career, Lillard coming back as a 36-year-old franchise goat, and Ja Morant having a history of off-court debacles.

Injury Projection D

  • The trailblazers had fantastic injury luck last year regarding their two most important defensive players, Toumani Camara and Donovan Clingan. Outside of that, a lot of their guards experienced injury misfortune and some of their key starters missed time, so they’re hoping for more luck here.
  • Damian Lillard, Ja Morant, and Rob Williams have famously suffered from injuries keeping them sidelined in recent years, so there’s high risk here the trailblazers may yet again experience some misfortune. The blazers are a fairly deep team, so there is a chance some rotational players can step up and fill in when needed, but I’m not too optimistic for a fully healthy season.

Nothing is given, but this roster should know that better than most.
      ### 2. New York Knicks [Last Season: 3 Seed, 53-29] Coming off of a dominant playoffs en route to their first NBA Championship in 53 years, many consider the Knicks as a shoe-in for the 1 seed. I am conserving some gusto on this front, noting that Mitchell Robinson played a huge role for their success, and projecting a bit of a “championship hangover” for a roster that has relied heavily on high starter minutes over the last few years. Not much has changed in the lineup, so we can expect the Knicks to continue to put themselves in tough situations game-to-game, regularly requiring large comebacks and the ability to elevate in the clutch. They’ll still be great. They’re still a team to fear in the postseason.
      ### Eastern Tier 2: Dogfight of Great Teams
      ### 3. Cleveland Cavaliers [Last Season: 4 Seed, 52-30] The Cavs run it back, completely sure that they just need to find the right wing to harmonize their elite guard duo with their elite frontcourt duo. I’d give it a 50% chance Peyton Watson is that wing, positioned well to break out with established defensive covering and flashes of explosive two-way potential to fulfill the Cavs’ hopes and dreams. The Cavs are a funny team as they’ve experienced an abundance of clear success in the last few years, yet this regularity has caused them to be overlooked in some ways. Kenny Atkinson may not fully understand how analytics works, but I would blindly put my faith in his formula for regular season wins nonetheless.
      ### 4. Detroit Pistons [Last Season: 1 Seed, 60-22] Since when was projecting the second youngest (weighted average by minutes played last season) team, who also dominated the conference winning 4 more games than the second seed, to now be a top 4 seed a hot take? In my opinion, many analysts are overreacting to the summer Duren drama and the infusion of talent into top Eastern Conference teams by plummeting the Pistons down the standings. Yes, they lost some frontcourt depth which helped them embrace their physically bruising identity last season, but I like their moves around the margin and I feel fine expecting their young core to continue impressing. Cade, bring home that MVP for my fantasy team.
      ### 5. Philadelphia 76ers [Last Season: 7 Seed, 45-37] Welcome to the show, Jalen Brown and LeBron James! Projecting the 76ers regular season success feels like a departure into madness as they boast the league’s most talented starting 5 on paper, yet feel destined for injury misfortune. The overwhelming talent and respectable depth should be enough for this team to find success all season, although I find a top seed prediction too risky in this Eastern Conference bloodbath. They care more that everyone will be healthy for the playoffs, anyhow. What feels more likely is that this team will host the most storylines throughout the season… I can’t wait to watch them hoop!
      ### 6. Indiana Pacers [Last Season: 14 Seed, 19-63] I hear you that a 14th seed swinging right back up to a 6th seed sounds optimistic. But, I believe in the magic and offensive identity that Tyrese Haliburton adds to this Pacers roster, expecting a huge turnaround from their 29th ranked offense last season. Maybe it’s blind faith, but I like this team’s continuity, elite coaching, and vast potential for any young player to break out out of nowhere. Zubac is a perfect fit for this roster with sneaky experience. It will be interesting to finally see how this Pacers rendition will perform with some real expectations going into the start of the season.
      ### 7. Toronto Raptors [Last Season: 5 Seed, 46-36] Adding Kawhi Leonard to this previously underrated roster may be a concoction for him to form the best duo in the league alongside star Scottie Barnes. Already a top 5 defense, adding the steady release valve of Kawhi’s effortless bucket-getting to this top 15 offense should mean this is a team of assassins. In the event of injury misfortune, a rational prediction given Kawhi’s history, they are rolling the dice that their young depth can step up to the challenge, which makes me a little hesitant to put them too high in the standings. Losing Mamu is tough, but they still have an ace in head coach Darko Rajakovic. You can tell my head is spinning on where exactly they’ll fall when all is said and done.
      ### 8. Miami Heat [Last Season: 10 Seed, 43-39] Giannis Antetokounmpo, referred to as the “Greek Freak” is an unstoppable force, statline dominating, absolutely winning player. Adding a top ~5 player like him to a consistently respectable roster with the best coach in the league spells horror for the rest of the Eastern Conference. That being said, the Heat did give up a lot to get him, something the Nets or Suns (not a sneak diss, KD, please don’t @ me) or Bucks or Sixers could tell you does not guarantee immediate success. The Heat should stand as an authoritative force on defense, with only above-average to all-time defenders across their starting five. Unfortunately, the modern NBA requires offense and specifically reliable 3-point production to excel in the win column. Giannis may average 30 points per game again, and we may casually anticipate the random 83 point scoring outburst from Bam Adebayo, but I retain my doubts that the Heat will regularly produce enough offense to win regularly in close finishes. (PS, I respect both of you, Klay and Timmy, and would be absolutely delighted to watch you guys rip nets all season).
      ### Eastern Tier 3: Play-in or 3 Lottery Balls
      ### 9. Atlanta Hawks [Last Season: 6 Seed, 46-36] I’m very excited to see the Hawks’ first full season under the stewardship of breakout star Jalen Johnson. With a multitude of promising young talent to build off of the base provided by fantastic off-season acquisitions in the form of ever-reliable role players Aaron Wiggins, Lu Dort, and Dorian Finney-Smith, there’s a chance this team catches flight (I’m so sorry) and breaks into Tier 1. The Hawks were a team looking for short term stability given small injury misfortune and the undertaking of finding their way in the post-Trae Young era last season, so I believe there should only be room for good vibes in the locker room this year. My projected drop in their seed this year is a pure reflection of the influx of off-season success in other Eastern Conference teams. Optimistically, this means with good luck the Hawks will have a real opening to deliver blows to higher seeded injury-riddled teams in the playoffs. NAW, you may not be known by the average human, but you’re a star to me.
      ### 10. Orlando Magic [Last Season: 8 Seed, 45-37] Although it feels like we’re many years into this project, Paolo Banchero and Franz Wagner are still young. I view them as very consistent players when healthy, and the roster has not experienced essentially any overturn, so we should logically anticipate a very similar looking season to last year. That is, until you realize the East got better across the board, notably including a few of their Southeastern Division rivals they have to play multiple times this year. Even if the Magic finally experience a fully healthy season for their starters, I question if it’s enough to make noise in the East. I’m not naïve enough to predict the Magic will finally figure out an effective offense with the same exact cast just because they have a new head coach. Although, their respectable defense and consistent pulse should equate to enough regular season wins to avoid irrelevancy.
      ### 11. Charlotte Hornets [Last Season: 9 Seed, 44-38] It’s no mystery to me why Charlotte experienced their most magical, successful season in a long time during Lamelo Ball’s healthiest campaign since his sophomore, all-star rendition. Orbiting around Lamelo’s offensive gravity, many young and auxiliary players stepped up in their roles netting the Hornets a top 5 offense and top 11 defense in 2025-26. There would be absolutely no reason to get cold feet on this fresh roster + coach matrimony unless the Hornets completely doubt Lamelo will ever be this healthy again. What sounds like a negative summary has more than just a silver lining. I love the way young players Kon Knueppel, Moussa Diabaté, Ryan Kalkbrenner, and Sion James stepped up last year, and I’m starting to gain real trust in their drafting savvy now seeing them select Christian Anderson Jr. and Hannes Steinbach– both of whom are awesome, by the way. Naz Reid is a fan favorite, and the other experience they’ve brought in should hold them in a somewhat competitive position. Ultimately, trading your superstar for only role players will enact regression in the short term, and this roster needs time before it produces magic again.
      ### 12. Washington Wizards [Last Season: 15 Seed, 17-65] I too am buying into the hype that adding floor raisers Trae Young and Anthony Davis will instantly improve a young roster. Wouldn’t it be nice if the Washington Wizards became a team to finally possess some cause for belief? They have an overabundance of young potential, and I think the start of this campaign will be paramount to the crucial near-term decisions they need to make. This roster may look different yet again by the end of this year or next, but I have faith that if they make intelligent decisions regarding how to best build around AJ Dybantsa’s superstar potential, they may eventually become a fearsome squad. Head coach Brian Keefe will be on the hot seat if the Wizards fail to eclipse 20 regular season wins for the fourth-straight season. Don’t let me down.
      ### 13. Chicago Bulls [Last Season: 12 Seed, 31-51] The franchise that has truly experienced it all. Untouchable in the 90’s, unlucky in the early 2010’s, unrelentingly mid in the early 2020’s, and now– at long last– just plain old bad. The Bulls waited too long to blow things up, as new draft lottery rules now mean they need to win just enough games to get a higher chance at a top pick. I like the additions of Norman Powell, Nic Claxton, and Buddy Hield for this reason. Matas Buzelis and Caleb Wilson will both take awhile to fully develop anyways, so why not add a few non-invasive vets? With the cleanest near-future draft portfolio, owning all of their own 1st- and 2nd-round round picks over the next 7 years, the Bulls are in a surprisingly comfy spot. Just hold on, find which playstyles and player archetypes jive well together, and plan a tactical strike when the team has a chance to compete. Definitely don’t rush into something stupid.
      ### Eastern Tier 4: Finding a Silver Lining
      ### 14. Brooklyn Nets [Last Season: 13 Seed, 20-62] The Nets can no longer be considered the younger, less handsome brother of the New York Knicks. With what happened in the 2025-26 season, they have been pushed so far into the Knicks’ shadow they may as well fully embrace the dark side. I’d actually like it if the new demotion zone this year meant we could let Jordi Fernández draft his own team of players and give them a shot. If there’s a silver lining to this season, it’s that the Nets have an incredible haul of upcoming first round draft picks. Although this current front office’s draft selections seem to have only misses like a NAV album, I will give the young guys the grace of time to find their way. There’s a real chance Mikel Brown and Egor Dëmin could produce sparks in the dark if they find their rhythm.
      ### 15. Milwaukee Bucks [Last Season: 11 Seed, 32-50] Despite their players currently looking like a Love Island cast, the Bucks did not have a long, hot summer. Actually, they decided with haste that a 32 win season was too balmy for their northern tastes and quickly traded franchise hero Giannis Antetokounmpo for, well, a Herro of some sort. To those who haven’t yet taken a deep dive into this roster’s contents, it might sound a tad mean to say the Bucks will have to scrap and fight every game they can to not be one of the worst three teams in the league. Oh wait, they don’t even own their own first round draft pick this year (nor in 2029). So, wins to them are like volleyballs to a castaway: rarely encountered and largely unhelpful anyhow. Brayden Burries should have a long and impactful NBA career, ideally for a more inspiring team, so if you guys could trade him when the Gary Tent inquisition leaves you desperate that’d be great, thanks. Winter is coming.
